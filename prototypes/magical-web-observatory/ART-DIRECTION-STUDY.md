# Art-direction study

Status: decided — Editorial Atlas
Date: 2026-08-24

## Question

Which visual language gives Observatory a distinctive, ownable identity rather
than reproducing the purple gradients, neon bloom and glass panels common to
AI-generated interfaces?

## Fixed product model

All variants use native SVG/CSS, the same six-Goal and thirty-Agent fixture,
Spatial Focus navigation and the same Attention Queue. Only the complete visual
system changes.

## Variants

1. `instrument` uses warm black, bone, brass and mineral pigments; circular
   field-instrument geometry; restrained illumination; and solid surfaces.
2. `atlas` uses a light paper field, editorial typography, flat cartographic
   colour, contour marks and printed-reference panels.
3. `industrial` uses graphite, steel, a restrained status palette, octagonal
   systems, reticles, grid structure and monospaced instrumentation.

These differ in typography, geometry, material, icon language, depth and colour
semantics. They are not palette swaps.

## Evaluation

- Does it remain recognisably Observatory with the name and logo cropped out?
- Does colour communicate operational state rather than ambient decoration?
- Does the world still feel magical without relying on purple neon bloom?
- Are Goals distinguishable through form and markings as well as colour?
- Do the Attention Queue and spatial world feel like one designed system?
- Does the visual language remain legible at portfolio and focused zoom?

## Verdict

Choose Editorial Atlas. It retains the spatial metaphor without presenting
Observatory as futuristic infrastructure software or a space game. Instrument
remained too space-oriented and Industrial was rejected outright.

Refine Atlas toward an ownable cartographic system rather than generic beige AI
branding. Mineral Ledger won the palette comparison and is now the sole active
Atlas implementation, with light and dark maps.
