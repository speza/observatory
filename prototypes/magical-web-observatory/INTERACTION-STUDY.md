# Interaction study

Status: decided — Spatial Focus plus Attention Queue
Date: 2026-08-24

## Question

Which interaction model turns the spatial universe into the clearest control
surface for supervising concurrent work?

## Controlled scenario

All variants use native SVG/CSS and the same fixture: six Goals, twenty-six
assigned Agents, four unassigned Agents, five human-attention or waiting states,
stale observations and explicit delegation relationships.

The variants deliberately disagree about structure:

1. `focus` makes the spatial world the navigation system. Selecting a Goal
   moves into it and progressively reveals Agent detail.
2. `deck` keeps attention, the spatial map and selected detail visible as three
   persistent operational columns.
3. `spotlight` makes the attention queue primary and treats the universe as
   retained context for one decision at a time.

## Evaluation

- Find what needs human attention within five seconds.
- Explain an Agent's purpose, state and current activity without opening it.
- Move from portfolio to Goal to Agent without losing spatial context.
- Notice stale or uncertain host facts without treating them as current truth.
- Understand a parent-child delegation relationship.
- Stay readable at the full fixture density and under zoom.

## Verdict

Use Spatial Focus as the primary product model. It preserves the universe as
the navigation and orientation surface. Add an Attention Queue as a supporting
lens for actionable and uncertain work; selecting an item returns directly to
its Agent in the spatial world.

Keep the dense Command Deck concept only as a potential secondary Operations
lens. Do not make a flat list the primary representation of all work.
